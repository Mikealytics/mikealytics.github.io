Enterprise leadership

From a lunch-and-learn
to shared practice.

At Cisco, I’ve helped drive AI adoption across our global Customer Experience organization by bringing people together, giving them a practical starting point, and building ways to keep learning.

01 / Community

Build a place people want to learn.

I started Cisco’s internal AI Innovator Hub for Project Managers with a lunch-and-learn. It grew into a community connecting thousands of people across Cisco around real questions and practical experiments.

The community grew through colleagues who shared their work, volunteered their time, and helped others make connections. Shared learning became something people could come back to.

The community story +

My contribution: Founding the Hub and bringing people together around practical AI learning.

The shared effort: Volunteer contributors and colleagues across Cisco made the examples, conversations, and connections possible.

The result: A recurring learning community. I reported 7,400 members in September 2026. That figure describes community membership.

02 / Shared methods

Give people a way into the work.

I led a global, cross-functional team creating the AI for PM Playbook: a practical starting point for applying AI to project and program management. Other CX roles used the approach to create their own playbooks.

How the Playbook fits +

The task was to connect AI possibilities to the work people already understood. My role was leading the global team and helping turn existing practice into a shared approach people could use.

The Playbook was a team effort. Its value as a model was giving other roles an approach they could adapt to their own work.

Performance reviews ask people to gather evidence and explain their contribution. Devarsh Pandya and I coauthored a practical AI prompt guide to help organize that work while keeping the person responsible for its accuracy.

One of three winners in Cisco’s AI use-case contest, from 333 submissions.

Thought leadership

Prompt less.
Practice more.

My talks have moved from exploring AI as a teammate to building the judgment to use it well. The thread is practical: choose useful work, supply the right evidence, and own what comes back.

01

Treat AI like a teammate.

In Berlin, I explored how people could begin working with AI. Give it the context a teammate would need, then stay involved in the work.

02

Address the capacity gap.

In Nashville, the executive conversation focused on capacity and the work AI could help take on. Start with the pressure people actually feel.

03

Put AI to work.

The PMI webinar brought the conversation into real applications. A useful example gives people a place to start and something they can judge.

04

Build judgment through practice.

“Prompt Less, Practice More” brings attention to framing, evidence, narrow permissions, and review. The prompt is a handoff in a larger work loop.

The Work Loop

Three jobs. A repeatable way to work.

  1. 01

    Frame the Work

    Choose the task and the context it needs.

  2. 02

    Guide the AI

    Make a clear handoff.

  3. 03

    Own the Result

    Inspect, correct, and decide.

Explore the eight moves +

Frame the Work

  1. Goal: What needs to change?
  2. Audience: Who will use the result?
  3. Sources: What evidence belongs here?
  4. Context: What does the AI need to understand?

Guide the AI

  1. Prompt / The Handoff: Give the task, expectations, and useful constraints.

Own the Result

  1. Review: Judge the result against the work.
  2. Refine: Carry a correction forward.
  3. Boundary: Keep human decisions and permissions clear.

Try it on one recurring task. Choose work you can judge. Define the evidence and boundaries. Review what comes back. Save one correction for the next attempt.

Meeting Insight Orchestrator

A local workflow for bringing Webex meeting recordings and transcripts into a workspace, then preparing context for an approved external AI tool.

The project includes meeting filters, a local cache, prompt templates, and bulk export. It makes the handoff visible so the person can decide what to share and where to take it.
